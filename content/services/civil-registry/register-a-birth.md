# Register a Birth

How to register a birth at the Office of the Municipal Civil Registrar, on time or late.

---

## Where to go

- **Office of the Municipal Civil Registrar:** (044) 760-4599. Ground floor, Main Building, Municipal Hall.
- **Office of the Municipal Treasurer:** 0925-305-3076. Ground floor, Main Building, Municipal Hall.

A birth must be registered in the municipality where it happened, within 1 month. The form is the Certificate of Live Birth (Municipal Form No. 102).

Fees are paid at the Municipal Treasurer's Office, which the document calls the MTO. You then bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office. The Treasurer's [Citizen's Charter](https://paombong.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Treasurer_s-Office-Citizen_s-Charter.pdf) says to bring the assessment stub from the civil registrar when you pay.

Where the document prints PSA, CCR, LCR as the source of a certificate, this page says the PSA or a civil registrar. The PSA is the Philippine Statistics Authority.

## Register a birth on time

For a birth that is registered within 1 month. The office records the Certificate of Live Birth, gives it a registry number and signs it.

- **Who can apply:** The document owner, or an authorized representative with a special power of attorney (SPA) or authorization letter and the valid government-issued IDs of both. The document says they must be 18 years old and above.
- **Fee:** The document prints no amount for registering a birth, although its steps include paying a fee. The table's only amounts are Php 200.00 marked "burial" and Php 200.00 marked "transfer", and its total row prints Php 400.00. The page on registering a death covers those. Ask the office what you will be charged.
- **Processing time:** 46 minutes, the printed total.

### What to bring

- **Accomplished Certificate of Live Birth.** Where to get it: The hospital, the lying-in clinic or a civil registrar.
- **The parents' certificate of marriage and government-issued valid ID.** Where to get it: The PSA or a civil registrar.
- **If the parents are not married and the father acknowledges the child: the notarized Affidavit of Acknowledgement and the Affidavit to Use the Surname of the Father (AUSF), 4 original copies.** Where to get it: A civil registrar and a notary public.
- **4 photocopies of the parents' government-issued IDs, with countersignatures.** Where to get it: The agency that issued each ID.

### Steps

1. Submit the Certificate of Live Birth with the requirements. If they are complete, the office records the document, assigns a registry number and signs it.
2. Pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer's Office (MTO) and bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office for recording.
3. Receive the registered document.

If the requirements are not complete, the office gives you a checklist and asks you to submit the rest.

## Register a birth late

For a birth that was not registered within 1 month. The document calls this delayed registration.

- **Who can apply:** The document owner, or an authorized representative with a special power of attorney (SPA) or authorization letter and the valid government-issued IDs of both. The document says they must be 18 years old and above.
- **Fee:** The document prints no amount for a delayed registration of birth, although its steps include paying a fee. The table's only amounts are Php 200.00 marked "burial" and Php 200.00 marked "transfer", and its total row prints Php 400.00. The page on registering a death covers those. Ask the office what you will be charged.
- **Processing time:** 46 minutes is the printed total at the office. Delayed registrations are released after a 10-day posting period.

### What to bring

- **PSA No Record of Birth (original and photocopy).** Where to get it: PSA.
- **Baptismal certificate.** Where to get it: The church where the child was baptized.
- **School record (Form 137) or school certification.** Where to get it: The school.
- **Record of vaccination, Comelec voter's record.** Where to get it: A hospital, clinic or rural health unit, for the vaccination record.
- **PhilHealth MDR, National ID, other valid IDs.** Where to get it: PhilHealth, for the MDR.
- **The parents' certificate of birth, or certificate of death.** Where to get it: The PSA or a civil registrar.
- **The parents' certificate of marriage and valid IDs.** Where to get it: The PSA or a civil registrar, and the agencies that issued the IDs.
- **Affidavit for Delayed Registration of Birth, by the registrant or the parents.** Where to get it: A notary public.
- **Affidavit of Two Disinterested Persons, with valid IDs.** Where to get it: A notary public.
- **Barangay certification, certificate of residency.** Where to get it: Your barangay.
- **2x2 ID picture.** You provide this.
- **Accomplished Certificate of Birth.** Where to get it: The hospital, the lying-in clinic or a civil registrar.
- **If a representative files: an SPA or authorization letter from the document owner, with the government-issued valid IDs of both.** Where to get it: The document owner and a notary public.

### Steps

1. Submit the accomplished Certificate of Birth with the requirements. If they are complete, the office records the document, assigns a registry number and signs it.
2. Pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer's Office (MTO) and bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office for recording.
3. Receive the registered document after the 10-day posting period.

The document lists these papers without saying which ones can be left out, so ask the office which apply to your case. If the requirements are not complete, the office gives you a checklist and asks you to submit the rest.
