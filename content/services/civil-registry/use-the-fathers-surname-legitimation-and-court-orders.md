# Use the Father's Surname, Legitimation and Court Orders

How the Office of the Municipal Civil Registrar records an acknowledgement of paternity, the use of the father's surname, a legitimation, and a court order such as an annulment or an adoption.

---

## Where to go

- **Office of the Municipal Civil Registrar:** (044) 760-4599. Ground floor, Main Building, Municipal Hall.
- **Office of the Municipal Treasurer:** 0925-305-3076. Ground floor, Main Building, Municipal Hall.

Fees are paid at the Municipal Treasurer's Office, which the document calls the MTO. You then bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office. The Treasurer's [Citizen's Charter](https://paombong.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Treasurer_s-Office-Citizen_s-Charter.pdf) says to bring the assessment stub from the civil registrar when you pay.

## Acknowledgement of paternity and use of the father's surname

Republic Act 9255 allows a child whose parents are not married to use the father's surname. The paper for it is the Affidavit to Use the Surname of the Father (AUSF). This is for a birth that is already registered.

- **Who can apply:** The parents and the document owner. The document says they must be 18 years old and above, but its checklist also covers children under 18, as the age groups below show. A child who is 7 years old or above signs the AUSF, attested by the mother.
- **Fee:** Php 200.00. You also cover the courier fee.
- **Processing time:** 64 minutes.

### What to bring

- **PSA Certificate of Live Birth of the child (original and 4 photocopies).** Where to get it: PSA.
- **If the child is registered under the mother's surname and without the father's name: a notarized Affidavit of Acknowledgement or Paternity by the father, with his government-issued valid ID (2 original copies and 2 photocopies with countersignature).** Where to get it: A notary public.
- **If the child is 0 to 6 years old: a notarized AUSF by the mother, with the attestation.** Where to get it: A civil registrar, then a notary public.
- **If the child is 7 to 17 years old: the AUSF signed by the child, with the mother's attestation.** Where to get it: A civil registrar.
- **If the child is 18 years old or above: the AUSF signed by the child.**
- **Government-issued valid IDs of the parents and of the child.** Where to get it: The agency that issued each ID. A school ID is listed for the child.

### Steps

1. Submit all the requirements. If they are complete, the office interviews the parents, then prepares the documents, assigns a registry number and signs them for submission to the PSA Central Office in Manila by courier.
2. Pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer's Office (MTO) and bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office for recording. You also cover the courier fee.
3. Receive your copy. You present it when you request a copy with annotations at the PSA.

## Legitimation

Legitimation by subsequent marriage is for a child whose parents were not married when the child was born and who married each other afterwards. This is for a birth that is already registered.

- **Who can apply:** The parents and the document owner. The document says they must be 18 years old and above.
- **Fee:** Php 200.00 is the fee printed for this service. The Treasurer's document lists Php 300.00 for "application for marriage and legitimation", so confirm the amount. You also cover the courier fee.
- **Processing time:** 64 minutes.

### What to bring

- **PSA Certificate of Live Birth of the child (original and 4 photocopies).** Where to get it: PSA.
- **If the child is registered under the mother's surname with the father's name unknown: a notarized Affidavit of Acknowledgement or Paternity by the father, with his valid ID.** Where to get it: A notary public.
- **Notarized Joint Affidavit of Legitimation by the parents.** Where to get it: A notary public.
- **PSA Certificate of Marriage (original and 4 photocopies).** Where to get it: PSA.
- **PSA CENOMAR of the parents (original and 4 photocopies).** Where to get it: PSA.
- **The child's certificate of baptism (original and 4 photocopies).** Where to get it: The church where the child was baptized.
- **Government-issued valid IDs of the parents and of the child.** Where to get it: The agency that issued each ID. A school ID is listed for the child.
- **Other requirements, if necessary.**

### Steps

1. Submit all the requirements. If they are complete, the office interviews the parents, then prepares the documents, assigns a registry number and signs them for submission to the PSA Central Office in Manila by courier.
2. Pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer's Office (MTO) and bring the official receipt back to the civil registry office for recording. You also cover the courier fee.
3. Receive your copy. You present it when you request a copy with annotations at the PSA.

## Register a court order

For a court decision that affects a civil registry record. The document's title names cancellation, annulment or recognition, and adoption. The office checks the papers and forwards them to the PSA Central Office.

- **Who can apply:** The document owner, or an authorized representative with an authorization letter or SPA and the government-issued valid IDs of both. The document says they must be 18 years old and above.
- **Fee:** Php 1,000.00. You also cover the courier fee.
- **Processing time:** 77 minutes.

### What to bring

- **The court's decision, Certificate of Finality, Certificate of Authenticity, Registration of Court Decrees and Decree of Absolute Nullity of Marriage (4 photocopies).** Where to get it: The court, or the civil registrar of the place where the court is.
- **The document owner's government-issued valid ID (4 photocopies with countersignature).** Where to get it: The agency that issued the ID.
- **If a representative applies: an SPA or authorization letter, with the government-issued valid IDs of both (4 copies with countersignature).** Where to get it: The document owner and a notary public.

### Steps

1. Submit the documents. The staff check that they are complete and authentic, then prepare and sign the documents to be forwarded to the PSA Central Office.
2. Pay the fee at the Municipal Treasurer's Office (MTO) and bring the official receipt back. The office registers it in its logbook. You also cover the courier fee.
3. Receive your copy. You present it when you request a copy with annotations at the PSA.

The document lists these court papers together and does not say which ones go with which kind of case, so ask the office what yours needs.
